1. Test whether an independently discovered oral-cholera-vaccine microbiome signature transfers to MucoRice
Why this is the most interesting pivot

The 2021 MucoRice phase-I study already reported that responders and nonresponders differed in baseline gut microbiota, but its metagenomic analysis was only 20 people: 12 responders and 8 nonresponders. The paper itself reported higher β-diversity among responders and differences involving Bacteroides, E. coli and Shigella. 
ResearchGate
+1

That means simply discovering "another bacterium associated with MucoRice response" is weak novelty.

A much stronger question became available with a newer independent OCV study: a 2025/2026 Nature Communications study analyzed baseline stool metagenomes from 89 recipients of a whole-cell killed oral cholera vaccine containing recombinant CTB, using a gene-level approach and reported associations involving sphingolipid-producing bacteria. 
Nature
+1

So the genuinely interesting test is:

Does a microbiome feature learned without looking at MucoRice predict MucoRice response?

That is an external-transfer experiment rather than another within-cohort association hunt.

Exact estimand

Pre-register:

Primary estimand: AUROC for a frozen, externally derived microbiome score in discriminating MucoRice CTB responders from nonresponders among the 20 participants with baseline metagenomes.

Secondary:

Spearman correlation between frozen score and continuous CTB-IgA/IgG response.

Calibration/slope, if sample size permits.

Difference in AUROC between the external signature and simple baseline diversity.

Do not optimize the signature on the MucoRice data.

The original paper's:

rather than an unconstrained modern ML model.

ResearchGate

A fair computational comparator would therefore be:

—not XGBoost with hundreds of tuned features.

Preregistered PASS/FAIL

PASS only if:

external score AUROC ≥ 0.75;

95% CI is reported;

it beats the prespecified diversity comparator by at least 0.10 AUROC;

direction of the association agrees with the external training hypothesis;

no MucoRice-derived feature selection was performed.

Otherwise FAIL as a new predictive discovery, although the result remains useful as a negative replication.

Given n=20, I would not demand p<0.05 as the sole gate; the confidence interval and effect size matter much more.

What would actually be new?

A result such as:

"A microbiome signature discovered independently in recipients of a conventional oral cholera vaccine transfers to MucoRice-CTB recipients despite differences in vaccine composition and population."

That is substantially more interesting than:

"Bacteroides differs between MucoRice responders and nonresponders."

The latter is already essentially in the 2021 paper.

48-hour falsification test

Before building anything elaborate:

obtain the published MucoRice participant-level abundance table/metagenomic data;

Strongest fair comparator

The strongest comparator is actually the published descriptive analysis:

Preregistered PASS/FAIL

PASS as a method contribution only if:

every variance component is supported by real replicate-level structure;

≥1 held-out production group can be predicted without refitting;

3. Re-estimate the human dose-response as a response-heterogeneity problem

This is the easiest to execute computationally but has the weakest novelty ceiling.

The 2021 trial randomized 60 healthy Japanese men into 1-, 3-, and 6-g MucoRice groups or matched placebo groups, 10 per arm; efficacy analysis lost three participants. 
ScienceDirect
+1

The product contained nominally:

1 g → 3 mg CTB

3 g → 9 mg CTB

6 g → 18 mg CTB

as reported in the trial materials. 
ResearchGate

The paper already found dose/time-dependent responses, particularly at 6 g. 
ScienceDirect

So "dose increases antibody response" is not a discovery.

The potentially new question is whether the published aggregate trajectory conceals a reproducible heterogeneity structure.

Exact estimand

Primary:

individual-level dose-response slope for log CTB-specific IgA and IgG, with the variance of the individual slopes.

Secondary:

whether a nonlinear model explains response trajectories better than a linear dose model, using prespecified AIC/BIC or cross-validated prediction error.

A possible estimand is:

Δlog(antibody)/Δlog(nominal CTB dose)

but only if individual-level observations can actually be recovered.

The MTBLS437 trap

I would explicitly exclude MTBLS437 from all three primary analyses.

The dataset is real and publicly deposited, but it is a 2017 metabolomic study of transgenic rice seed. The repository describes it as untargeted GC/TOF-MS metabolomics, and the paper states that its raw data are available under MTBLS437. 
PubMed Central (PMC)
+1

It is therefore useful for questions such as:

"Does MucoRice genetic modification alter seed metabolism?"

It is not a CTB-dose dataset and cannot independently validate an antigen-dose or human-immunogenicity claim.

Using it as "another MucoRice replicate" would recreate exactly the provenance error your guard is supposed to prevent.

What I would actually pursue
Primary project: Direction 1

The research question becomes:

Can an oral-cholera-vaccine microbiome signature discovered independently of MucoRice predict MucoRice-CTB immune response in an untouched cohort?

That has a clean causal chronology:

external discovery → locked algorithm → MucoRice test

rather than:

MucoRice data → feature hunting → MucoRice association → claim of discovery.

And it has a genuinely interesting possible outcome in either direction:

positive: evidence for a microbiome feature that generalizes across very different OCV platforms;

negative: evidence that the published microbiome-response association is vaccine/platform/population-specific.

Either is scientifically interpretable.

The particularly important point is that the 2025/2026 OCV work gives you a pre-existing biological hypothesis, not merely another dataset. Its authors explicitly developed a gene-level method linking baseline microbiome features to oral cholera-vaccine responses and identified sphingolipid-producing bacteria.
